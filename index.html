1. Context

Section 1 – Context

Where MB Fibreglass Stands Today

MB Fibreglass has supplied composite materials – resins, fibreglass, silicone and Jesmonite casting products – to trade and hobbyist buyers across the UK and Ireland since 1972. The audit set out to build a 3-month and 6-month SEO plan, measured against two conversion types tracked separately: purchases and form submissions.

Organic sessions have fallen 34% since the March 2026 peak (7,050 → 4,665 monthly sessions). Investigating that decline surfaced a live technical issue actively limiting how much of the site can be crawled at all.

At the same time, the business's active growth push into Jesmonite has almost no organic visibility, and a meaningful share of the existing catalogue is under-optimised or under-converting.

Guiding Principle

Stop the bleeding, then build the gain. Phase one resolves what's actively suppressing crawl and conversion today. Phase two builds the content and structure needed to capture demand the business is already investing in.

2. The Findings

Section 2 – The Findings

Three Issues, One Sequence

The audit surfaced three distinct problems, each requiring a different kind of fix, but with a clear order of dependency between them.

01 – Technical Infrastructure
Actively suppressing crawl and access
The site's security layer blocks any visiting IP after roughly 60 requests in 5 seconds, with no recovery during that session. It is plausible this same rule is throttling Googlebot, which would help explain the 34% traffic decline since March.
02 – Content & Structure
Significant on-page headroom
373 pages (29%) flagged as low-content, 97% of pages missing H2 structure, and duplicate titles/descriptions across variant product pages. Jesmonite – the active growth category – has almost no organic visibility to show for it.
03 – Mobile Checkout Friction
Losing buyers at the finish line
Mobile converts at 21% versus desktop's 29% sitewide. The gap concentrates specifically at basket (58.5% vs 46.2% bounce) and checkout (52.9% vs 39.6% bounce) – purchase-flow friction, not general browsing issues.
3. Scope

Section 3 – Scope

What the Programme Delivers

Each finding maps to a concrete workstream, sequenced so technical fixes unblock everything that follows.

Technical remediation

Resolve the rate-limit issue with the client's host, roll out H2 structure and meta/title fixes at template level, and correct sitemap hygiene errors.

Content & keyword strategy

A structured Jesmonite content hub modelled on the content-cluster approach driving a direct competitor's visibility, a rank-and-convert fix for pond-lining-kits.html, and keyword tracking rebuilt around the real catalogue.

Checkout UX review

A targeted audit of the mobile basket and checkout flow specifically – not a full site redesign – aimed squarely at closing the conversion gap on traffic the site already has.

4. The Roadmap

Section 4 – The Roadmap

Phased Plan of Action

Technical fixes come first because they unblock diagnosis and may be actively suppressing crawl today. Content and conversion work compound on top once the foundation is sound.

Phase 0 Weeks 1–2 Escalate the WAF rate-limit issue with the host; fix sitemap and URL errors; confirm crawl recovers.
Phase 1 Month 1 Template-level technical fixes: H2 structure, title/meta cleanup, keyword tracking rebuild.
Phase 2 Month 2–3 Jesmonite content hub build-out, pond-lining-kits.html optimisation, revenue-weighted low-content page remediation.
Phase 3 Month 4–6 Blog consolidation and expansion, mobile basket/checkout UX fixes, ongoing monitoring and competitor benchmarking.
Why technical comes first
It's blocking further diagnosis, it may be actively suppressing Googlebot's crawl today, and nothing else in the plan pays off fully until it's resolved.
5. Phase 1
Phase 1 – Month 1

Technical Foundation

Fix what's actively limiting crawl and visibility, and rebuild the measurement foundation everything else depends on.

01 – WAF & crawl health

→Escalate the ~60-requests-in-5-seconds block to the hosting/security provider, with reproduction steps from this audit.
→Check server logs around March 2026 to confirm whether Googlebot was caught by the same rule.
→Fix the invalid /delivery[1].html sitemap entry and the 1970-epoch lastmod date on /blog/.

02 – Template-level on-page fixes

→Roll out H2 structure via the shared product/category template – resolves ~1,233 pages in a single change.
→Resolve the 25 duplicate titles and 108 duplicate meta descriptions, prioritising the RAL colour and bottle-size variant clusters.
→Rebuild the tracked keyword set around the real catalogue – Jesmonite, RTV silicone, pond lining – replacing the current generic list.
6. Phase 2
Phase 2 – Month 2–3

Content & Keyword Growth

Build visibility for the category the business is already investing in, and fix the clearest existing rank-and-convert gap.

01 – Jesmonite content hub

→Build a structured how-to/explainer hub for Jesmonite, modelled on the content-cluster approach driving a direct competitor's /learning/ visibility.
→Target the currently near-invisible core terms (jesmonite pigment and related) alongside the existing jesmonite_ac100_kit.html revenue page.
→Expand the four blog posts that already pull traffic (mixing-by-hand-vs-blade, consolidation rollers, cleaning resin brushes, carbon fibre) into this hub.

02 – Pond lining & low-content remediation

→Rewrite pond-lining-kits.html and improve internal linking to close the gap between page-3 rankings and real demand (210/mo per term).
→Triage the 373 low-content pages by GA4 revenue contribution; fix the highest-value pages first.
The double opportunity
Pond-lining-kits.html already gets 1,416 sessions/year but only 70 conversions. It needs both better rankings and better on-page conversion copy – fixing one without the other leaves value on the table.
7. Phase 3
Phase 3 – Month 4–6

Conversion & Consolidation

With the technical and content foundation in place, the focus shifts to converting existing traffic and tidying what no longer earns its keep.

Blog consolidation

  • Cull or redirect the 40+ dormant 2012–2014 posts earning 0–2 sessions a year.
  • Fold the four proven performers into the Jesmonite/technique content hub.

Mobile checkout UX

  • Targeted review of mobile basket and checkout: form layout, payment options, button sizing.
  • Aimed at closing the bounce gap at basket (46%→59%) and checkout (40%→53%).

Monitoring & benchmarking

  • Monthly rank tracking against the rebuilt keyword set.
  • GA4 conversion tracking against both purchases and form submits, tracked separately.
  • Ongoing benchmarking against easycomposites.co.uk and other tracked competitors.

Why a content hub, not just more blog posts

easycomposites.co.uk's /learning/ hub drives a large share of its 11,000+ organic keywords through a handful of structured how-to and explainer articles that each rank for multiple related longtail terms and cross-link to product pages. MB Fibreglass's existing blog has four posts already doing this on a small scale – the hub model extends that pattern deliberately, rather than adding more isolated posts.

8. Prioritisation

Section 5 – Prioritisation

How We'll Decide What Comes First

With no Google Search Console access, prioritisation leans more heavily on two sources than it otherwise would: commercial data from GA4, and keyword/competitor data from Ubersuggest.

01Commercial value – GA4

Sessions, conversions and revenue per landing page, tracked against both purchases and form submits separately. This ensures effort is weighted towards pages that convert, not just pages that attract traffic.

02Keyword opportunity – Ubersuggest

Search volume, current ranking position and competitor gaps for the real catalogue. Pages ranking just off page one, or completely absent for high-volume terms like "silicone moulding" (4,400/mo), are where content work moves the needle fastest.

The combined priority list
Rank pages by GA4 commercial value, then weight by keyword headroom in Ubersuggest. A page with real revenue, a page-two ranking and clear search volume is the strongest candidate. We also recommend setting up Bing Webmaster Tools early in Phase 1 to begin recovering some of the query-level visibility a GSC connection would normally provide.

One note on timing. This analysis uses the site's current data, captured while the technical issue was still live. Once Phase 1's fixes land and crawling recovers, we re-baseline in GA4 and Ubersuggest and let fresh performance drive Phase 2 and 3 prioritisation.

9. Rationale

Section 6 – Rationale

Why Sequence It This Way

Each phase depends on the one before it. Doing them out of order wastes effort on work that can't yet pay off.

Technical fixes unblock everything else – if Googlebot is caught by the same rate limit, no amount of content work recovers lost visibility until it's fixed.
Content and conversion work only pays off once crawling and indexing are healthy again – new Jesmonite content needs to actually be found.
The Jesmonite hub captures demand the business is already investing in, before a competitor's content strategy pulls further ahead.
Mobile checkout fixes convert traffic that's already arriving – the fastest route to incremental revenue without needing new visibility first.
The Outcome

Within 3 months, MB Fibreglass resolves the technical issue likely suppressing its crawl and traffic, fixes the on-page gaps affecting hundreds of pages, and tracks the keywords that actually matter. Within 6 months, the Jesmonite range has genuine organic visibility, the blog is consolidated into working content assets, and the mobile purchase flow converts closer to desktop's rate.
